Amazon Vendor Central is the invitation-only portal for first-party (1P) vendors who sell products wholesale directly to Amazon. Unlike Seller Central (3P), vendors sell to Amazon at wholesale cost, and Amazon resells as the merchant of record. Vendor Central offers premium content capabilities and direct retail partnerships but surrenders pricing control and margins to Amazon's buying team.

- **1P (First-Party) Vendor** — Brand selling wholesale to Amazon, which then sells to consumers; Amazon controls the retail price
- **PO (Purchase Order)** — Amazon issues purchase orders to vendors based on forecasted demand; vendors must confirm within 7 days
- **Co-op Fees** — Deductions from vendor invoices for marketing, freight, and other operational contributions Amazon charges
- **Chargeback** — Penalty Amazon deducts from vendor payments for packaging, labeling, or shipping compliance failures
- **VINE Program** — Amazon's early reviewer program available to Vendor Central brands for new product launches
- **Shortage Claim** — Amazon claim that fewer units were received than invoiced, triggering disputes requiring vendor documentation
- **Demand Forecast** — Amazon's predicted order quantities used for vendor production planning, available in Vendor Central
- **ARA (Amazon Retail Analytics)** — Business analytics tool in Vendor Central providing sales, inventory, and traffic data

Vendor relationships are invitation-only — Amazon contacts brands it wants to carry directly. Vendors negotiate a wholesale cost with Amazon's buying team and enter a vendor agreement specifying co-op terms, freight terms, and compliance requirements. Amazon then issues purchase orders as needed based on their demand forecasting.

Vendors must ship to Amazon's designated fulfillment centers within required windows, meeting strict packaging and labeling compliance. Failures trigger chargebacks — financial deductions on subsequent payments — for defective units, label errors, or late deliveries. Chargeback management requires systematic documentation and dispute processes.

Amazon sets the retail price based on their margin targets and competitive pricing algorithms. Vendors cannot control prices, and Amazon's price matching algorithms may reduce prices below what vendors consider brand-appropriate levels. MAP (minimum advertised price) policies are difficult to enforce in the 1P model.

Payment terms are typically net 60–90 days, meaning vendors wait 2–3 months after shipping to receive payment. Co-op deductions (marketing contributions, freight allowances, damage allowances) are taken from invoice payments, reducing net vendor revenue.

Enhanced content (A+ Content, Brand Stores) is accessible through Vendor Central. Vendor Central brands typically receive higher priority placement in Amazon promotions and merchandising programs compared to third-party sellers.

- Consumer brands with existing retail distribution adding Amazon as a direct retail account
- Large CPG companies maintaining 1P relationships while also managing 3P hybrid strategies
- Brands prioritizing retail partnership status and premium content access over pricing control
- Manufacturers preferring to sell in bulk to a single buyer rather than manage individual consumer orders
- Companies participating in Amazon Launchpad for new product introductions

| Advantage | Disadvantage |
|-----------|--------------|
| Amazon manages all consumer sales, returns, and customer service | No control over retail pricing; Amazon may discount below brand standards |
| Premium content and placement opportunities vs. 3P sellers | Co-op fees and chargebacks reduce effective margins significantly |
| Bulk PO sales simplify logistics vs. individual consumer order fulfillment | Invitation-only; brands cannot self-enroll |
| Amazon's demand forecasts support vendor production planning | Net 60–90 payment terms create cash flow challenges for smaller vendors |
| Credibility of "Sold by Amazon" listing builds consumer trust | Amazon can reduce or stop POs without notice, disrupting vendor revenue |
