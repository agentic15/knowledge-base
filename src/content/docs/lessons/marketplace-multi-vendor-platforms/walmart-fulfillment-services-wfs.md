Walmart Fulfillment Services (WFS) is Walmart's third-party logistics program allowing marketplace sellers to store inventory in Walmart's fulfillment network and have orders picked, packed, and shipped by Walmart. WFS listings earn a 2-day delivery tag that improves search ranking and conversion rates. The program is Walmart's answer to Amazon FBA, and its fees are competitive or lower than FBA for many product sizes.

- **2-Day Delivery Tag** — Visual indicator on WFS-fulfilled listings showing fast delivery availability; improves Buy Box and conversion
- **Fulfillment Fee** — Per-unit charge covering receiving, storage, pick/pack, and shipping through WFS
- **Storage Fee** — Monthly charge per cubic foot of inventory stored in Walmart fulfillment centers
- **Inventory Performance** — WFS metric tracking sell-through rate and identifying excess or slow-moving inventory
- **Prep Requirements** — Packaging and labeling standards for inventory sent to WFS; non-compliance causes check-in delays
- **Returns Processing** — WFS handles customer returns; returned items assessed as sellable or unsellable
- **WFS Eligibility** — Not all products are eligible; hazardous materials, oversized items, and temperature-sensitive products may be excluded
- **Replenishment Planning** — Managing inbound shipment frequency and quantity to maintain adequate WFS inventory levels

WFS operates similarly to Amazon FBA in its fundamental model. Sellers create inbound shipments through Seller Center, following Walmart's packaging and labeling requirements. Inventory ships to designated Walmart fulfillment centers. Upon receipt, Walmart scans inventory into their system and makes it available for fulfillment.

WFS fulfillment fees are charged per unit based on weight and dimensions, similar to FBA's size tiers. Monthly storage fees apply per cubic foot of occupied space. Walmart's storage fee structure does not currently have the aggressive long-term storage fees that Amazon applies after 365 days, making it relatively more forgiving for slower-moving inventory.

Payout cycles are generally faster than Amazon's — Walmart releases funds approximately 7–14 days after delivery, compared to Amazon's bi-weekly cycle. This improves cash flow for WFS sellers.

Product eligibility requires items to meet Walmart's size and category restrictions. Oversize items (generally over 150 lbs or exceeding dimensional limits), lithium battery products, and certain hazardous categories may be excluded from WFS or require specific compliance documentation.

Returns handled by WFS allow customers to return items to Walmart stores in addition to mail returns — a significant advantage for customers and a differentiator from Amazon, where store returns are limited. Returned items assessed as unsellable can be disposed of or removed at seller request.

- Marketplace sellers wanting Walmart 2-day delivery benefits without managing carrier relationships
- Amazon FBA sellers expanding to Walmart and wanting parallel fulfillment structure
- Sellers with standard-size, high-velocity products suited to Walmart's customer demographics
- Brands seeking retail-adjacent fulfillment to complement brick-and-mortar Walmart distribution
- Sellers wanting faster payouts than Amazon's bi-weekly settlement cycle

| Advantage | Disadvantage |
|-----------|--------------|
| 2-day delivery tag improves Buy Box performance and conversion rates | WFS fulfillment center network smaller than Amazon's; coverage and speed may vary |
| Competitive fees; often lower than FBA for standard-size products | Not all product categories and sizes are eligible for WFS |
| Faster payout cycle (7–14 days) vs. Amazon's bi-weekly settlement | Walmart's marketplace buyer volume is smaller than Amazon's |
| In-store returns capability improves customer experience | Inventory performance metrics require monitoring to avoid restrictions |
| Less aggressive long-term storage fees than Amazon FBA | WFS is newer program; seller support and documentation less mature than FBA |
