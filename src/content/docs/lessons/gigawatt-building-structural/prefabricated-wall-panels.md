Prefabricated wall panels are factory-manufactured wall assemblies delivered to site ready for installation, dramatically reducing field labor hours and construction duration on gigawatt-scale datacenter projects. By moving manufacturing off the critical path and into controlled factory conditions, prefabricated panels improve quality consistency, reduce weather delays, and allow parallel fabrication while foundations and structural steel proceed on site.

- **Insulated Metal Panel (IMP)** — factory-laminated composite with steel skins and polyurethane or mineral wool insulation core
- **Prefabricated Exterior Wall Panel** — a complete wall assembly including framing, insulation, sheathing, and sometimes finish
- **Shop Drawings** — detailed fabrication drawings produced by the panel manufacturer for engineering review
- **Tolerances** — allowable dimensional deviations in panel size, flatness, and connection hole locations
- **Panel Joints** — the connection and sealant system between adjacent panels ensuring weathertightness
- **Thermal Break** — an insulating material in panel connection hardware that prevents heat conduction through metal fasteners
- **Factory Acceptance Testing (FAT)** — quality checks performed at the factory before shipment
- **Erection Sequence** — the planned order of panel installation to maintain structural stability and alignment

On a 1 GW campus with multiple 100,000+ sq ft data halls, the exterior wall area can exceed 2 million square feet. Conventional stick-frame exterior wall construction at that scale would require enormous on-site labor forces and months of sequential work. Prefabricated IMPs or structural insulated panel systems shift production off the critical path, enabling factories to run multi-shift operations producing panels while foundation and structural steel work continues on site.

IMP systems—the most common choice—use a rigid foam insulation core (polyurethane or mineral wool) continuously bonded between profiled steel skins. Standard panels arrive in widths of 24–48 inches and can be cut to custom lengths up to 50+ feet. A crew of 4–6 workers with a crane can erect 3,000–6,000 square feet per day. The lapped male-female joint between panels provides a continuous thermal break and conceals fasteners, maintaining weathertightness without exposed sealant on the primary weather surface.

Larger precast concrete wall panels require separate engineering for the lifting hardware, crane radius, and panel placement sequence. Each concrete panel may weigh 50–100 tons, limiting delivery frequency and requiring precise site logistics. Precast is favored for security-critical zones and blast-rated sections where concrete mass provides performance impossible in metal panel systems.

Shop drawing review is essential: connection embedments in the structural steel must align precisely with factory-drilled holes in panels, and errors discovered after delivery require expensive field drilling or returned panels. BIM coordination between the panel supplier's model and the structural model eliminates most conflicts before fabrication begins.

- Primary exterior wall cladding on large data hall buildings
- Blast-rated perimeter walls around transformer and generator yards
- Interior data hall partition walls requiring fire ratings and acoustic attenuation
- Cold storage and controlled humidity rooms within the facility
- Utility buildings and generator enclosures requiring rapid erection

| Advantage | Disadvantage |
|-----------|--------------|
| Factory quality control exceeds field construction consistency | Lead times of 12–20 weeks require early design commitment |
| Rapid site erection compresses overall schedule | Damage during transport can delay a specific section of the building |
| Integrated insulation reduces on-site material handling | Panel systems have limited architectural flexibility compared to framed walls |
| All-weather installation possible; panels not sensitive to rain | Custom panel dimensions require long lead times and add cost |
